Your task is to extract reliable educational information from a lecture transcript.

The transcript may contain:
- speech recognition errors
- repetitions
- incomplete sentences
- informal explanations
- examples
- questions and answers
- technical terminology

Your goal is to identify the educational content actually communicated by the lecturer while preserving its meaning.

Extract the following:

1. Main subject
2. Major topics
3. Important concepts
4. Important definitions
5. Relationships between concepts
6. Examples used to explain concepts
7. Important facts, rules, or procedures
8. Questions raised or left unanswered

Factuality rules:

- Extract information only when it is supported by the transcript.
- Do not use general knowledge to fill missing information.
- Do not invent facts, definitions, examples, or relationships.
- Do not infer relationships, causes, comparisons, or conclusions that are not supported by the transcript.
- Do not combine separate statements into a new claim.
- Distinguish between statements made by the lecturer and examples used to illustrate those statements.
- If information is unclear because of transcription errors or incomplete speech, preserve the uncertainty instead of guessing.
- Correct obvious speech-to-text errors when the intended meaning is clear.
- Preserve important technical terminology.

Processing rules:

- Ignore conversational filler and unnecessary repetition.
- Prefer concise factual statements.
- Preserve important context needed to understand a concept.
- Do not turn concepts into unnecessarily long explanations.
- Organize the extracted information clearly so that another model can use it to generate study material.

**SOURCE GROUNDING AND UNCERTAINTY**

- Preserve the language used by the lecturer. Do not translate the lecture into another language unless explicitly requested.
- For each major topic, concept, definition, and conclusion, retain enough of the lecturer's original wording or context to verify its meaning.
- Prefer information explicitly explained by the lecturer over interpretations based on isolated words.
- When a passage is severely corrupted, do not reconstruct the intended explanation from surrounding context alone.
- Keep uncertain names, quotations, dates, and attributions uncertain unless the transcript clearly establishes them.
- Do not invent illustrative stories, settings, events, quotations, or examples.
- Distinguish the lecturer's interpretation from the underlying text, poem, code, or other material being discussed.
- If the transcript contains contradictory or potentially misrecognized information, preserve the discrepancy rather than silently choosing an interpretation.
- Prioritize the central subject and the lecturer's actual explanations over minor details and speculative interpretations.

**OUTPUT ORGANIZATION**

Organize the extracted information by major topic. Under each topic, separate:
1. Information explicitly stated in the lecture.
2. Examples or source material discussed.
3. Interpretations or conclusions expressed by the lecturer.
4. Uncertainties caused by transcription errors.

Do not force every category to contain information. Omit categories that are not relevant to the lecture.
